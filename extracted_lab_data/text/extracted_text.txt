About Teal Carbon Lab
The Teal Carbon Lab is the world’s first dedicated research initiative advancing the science of carbon dynamics in shallow inland (non-tidal) wetland ecosystems. Conceived under the visionary leadership of Professor Dr. Laxmi Kant Sharma, the lab reflects a long-standing commitment to conservation, scientific excellence, and sustainable development. Rooted in interdisciplinary research, the lab integrates biogeochemistry, hydrology, and GeoAI to address complex environmental challenges at regional and global scales.
Driven by the urgent need to understand and protect fragile inland wetlands, the Teal Carbon Lab focuses on ecosystems that remain critically understudied despite their significant role in global carbon storage and climate regulation. These systems are increasingly threatened by hydro-climatic variability, land-use change, and anthropogenic pressures, leading to accelerated carbon loss and greenhouse gas emissions.
The lab advances the concept of teal carbon as a process-based framework, capturing the dynamic interactions between carbon sequestration and methane emissions in freshwater wetland environments. By combining field-based observations, satellite data, and machine learning approaches, the lab generates high-resolution, policy-relevant insights into wetland carbon processes.
A central objective of the Teal Carbon Lab is to translate scientific understanding into actionable Nature-based Solutions (NbS) that are locally grounded and globally scalable. Emphasising indigenous and ecosystem-based approaches, the lab contributes to climate mitigation, ecological restoration, and sustainable resource management.
Through its Living Lab approach, the initiative bridges science, policy, and practice, positioning inland wetlands as critical natural climate solutions for addressing global environmental challenges and achieving long-term sustainability.

Our motivation
The conceptual foundation of the Teal Carbon Lab is inspired by the pioneering work of Dr. Amanda Nahlik and Professor Dr. Siobhan Fennessy, who first articulated the concept of teal carbon to highlight the critical role of shallow inland (non-tidal) wetlands in the global carbon cycle. Their research brought global attention to freshwater wetland ecosystems as significant, yet underrepresented, carbon reservoirs with complex biogeochemical dynamics.
Building on their foundational vision, our work seeks to expand and operationalise the teal carbon framework through advanced field measurements, remote sensing, and GeoAI-driven modelling. Their contributions have not only shaped the scientific understanding of inland wetland carbon processes but have also motivated the development of integrated approaches to quantify, monitor, and manage these ecosystems under changing climatic conditions.
The Teal Carbon Lab carries forward this legacy by transforming conceptual insights into actionable, science-based Nature-based Solutions, aimed at addressing pressing global challenges related to climate change, wetland degradation, and sustainable ecosystem management.

WHAT IS TEAL CARBON
“Teal carbon refers to carbon stored and cycled within all types of shallow inland (non-tidal) wetlands, including marshes, swamps, peatlands, and other lacustrine, riverine or palsutrine systems .”
“These ecosystems regulate climate through a complex balance of carbon sequestration and greenhouse gas emissions, particularly methane.”
“Recent global synthesis demonstrates that teal carbon ecosystems store hundreds of petagrams of carbon globally, with peatlands alone contributing the largest share, while simultaneously acting as dynamic sources of CH₄ under changing hydro-climatic conditions”
(Kumar et al., 2025)
“Unlike static carbon classifications, teal carbon represents a process-driven framework, capturing the coupled dynamics of hydrology, biogeochemistry, and ecosystem function.”
